Image Diff — Compare Two Images Pixel by Pixel

Drop two images onto the boxes below to see exactly what changed between them — every pixel that differs is highlighted, so a moved button, a shifted line of text, or a recolored icon jumps out instead of hiding in a side-by-side squint test. Comparison happens entirely on canvas in your browser; neither image is ever uploaded anywhere.

Spotting the difference between two images

Unlike the text tools here, this compares two images pixel by pixel and highlights where they differ — the visual equivalent of a diff. It's built for the "spot the change" cases where two pictures are meant to be identical but might not be.

What it's good for

  • Visual regression checks: a screenshot before and after a code change, to see exactly which pixels moved.
  • Export comparisons: confirming two renders or two compressions of the same image really match.
  • Catching subtle edits — a changed number on a graphic, a moved element — that the eye glosses over.

Both images are loaded and compared entirely in your browser; they're never uploaded, so screenshots of private work stay private. For best results, compare images of the same dimensions.

Common questions

What counts as a "different" pixel?

Each pixel's red, green, blue and alpha values are compared between the two images; if the combined difference passes a small threshold (to absorb harmless compression noise), it's marked as changed and highlighted in the result.

What if the two images are different sizes?

The comparison canvas is sized to the larger of the two images; any area only one image covers counts as fully different, so a resized or cropped image will show a highlighted border or region rather than failing outright.

Are my images uploaded anywhere?

No. Both images are decoded and compared entirely on an in-browser <canvas> element — nothing is sent to a server, and the comparison works offline once the page has loaded.

What image formats are supported?

Anything your browser can decode natively — PNG, JPEG, WebP, GIF (first frame) and SVG all work, since the comparison operates on decoded pixel data, not the file format itself.
